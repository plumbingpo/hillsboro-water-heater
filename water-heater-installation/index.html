Water heater installation covers more ground than a simple swap. Some Hillsboro, OR homeowners are finishing a basement or building an addition and need a second heater. Other jobs involve moving a unit out of a cramped closet, stepping up to a bigger tank for a growing family, or switching fuels entirely. Each of those jobs involves new gas piping, new vent routes, new circuits, or new water lines, and each one has to be planned before the first pipe is cut. Hillsboro Water Heater handles installations of every size, from a single point-of-use heater under a sink to a whole-house tankless system.

Before any pipe is cut, we size the heater to your household. For tanks, first-hour rating counts more than gallons; for tankless, it is gallons per minute at your winter temperature rise. Then we pick the location with you. Gas models require a vent route outdoors and adequate combustion air. Heat pump models require breathing room, a space that stays above roughly 40 degrees, and a place to send condensate. An electric tankless model often has to have its own high-amperage circuit. Anywhere a leak could ruin finished space, a drain pan goes underneath. You get clear choices and a firm price before work begins.

On installation day, nothing is left to guesswork. New gas piping is run and pressure-tested, a sediment trap goes on, and the vent is routed exactly to spec. Electric and heat pump units get a circuit matched to their load. Water lines get accessible shutoffs and proper metal transitions, and the relief valve discharge runs full size to a safe point. Code items like expansion tanks, drain pans, and seismic straps are added where required, and we handle the permit when your jurisdiction needs one. Symptoms That Mean It's Time to Call

Catching a symptom early is often the difference between a quick part swap and a tank that gives out on the garage floor. Keep an eye out for the following:

  • Big-tub or multi-shower demand the current heater cannot meet
  • An addition too far from the existing heater for quick hot water
  • Interest in tankless or heat pump efficiency
  • A water heater installed above finished rooms with no drain pan
  • Missing code items like an expansion tank, drain pan, or proper venting
  • New construction or a major renovation that needs a heater from scratch
  • Long waits for hot water at faucets far from the heater
  • A tank that cannot keep up with your household anymore

Why Professional Water Heater Installation Matters

Consider the gas side first. An undersized line starves a new heater whenever the furnace or range runs, and a whole-house tankless unit usually needs more gas than the existing line carries. The vent has to be the right material, size, and pitch, with proper clearances, so exhaust leaves the home instead of drifting back into it. Electric models bring their own math, since breaker, wire gauge, and panel capacity all have to match. Add relief valve piping, dielectric fittings, expansion tanks, drain pans, seismic straps, and permit rules that vary from one jurisdiction to the next, and there are a lot of details to get right.

Professional service handles every trade involved, not just setting the heater. We size by first-hour rating or flow rate, run and test the gas piping, route the vent to spec, match the circuit to the load, and add expansion tanks, drain pans, or straps where required. Gas units get a draft and carbon monoxide check before we leave. You end up with an installation that is safe, properly sized, and ready for inspection. If a smaller or simpler heater fits your needs better, you will hear it from us directly.

How to Prevent Water Heater Problems

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here's where to begin:

  • Do not box the heater in; leave room for service and airflow
  • Store paint, gasoline, and solvents far from any gas water heater
  • A 120-degree setting protects against burns and slows mineral buildup
  • Check the drain pan and relief valve discharge line now and then for signs of water
  • Schedule regular maintenance, including an anode rod check
  • Descale tankless units once a year, or more often with hard water
  • Start a flushing routine early, before sediment has a chance to harden

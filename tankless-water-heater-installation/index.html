Why do so many people switch to tankless? Mostly three things: hot water that keeps coming, lower standby energy use, and space. A tankless heater stays idle until you open a tap, then fires a high-output burner or electric element and heats the water in passing. With no stored water to keep warm, it wastes less energy between uses, and with regular descaling it can last two decades. It is not magic, though. Performance depends entirely on sizing and installation, which is where our attention goes on every Hillsboro, OR install.

Sizing is where tankless installs succeed or fail. We size by flow rate and temperature rise: add up the fixtures you realistically run at the same time in gallons per minute, then factor in how cold the incoming water gets in winter, since a unit that delivers five gallons per minute at a mild rise may deliver noticeably less when the groundwater is cold. Then comes the infrastructure. A whole-home gas unit can draw 150,000 to 199,000 BTU, which often means a larger gas line or a meter check. Venting must match the unit, and electric tankless models can need a large share of your electrical service, sometimes enough to require a panel upgrade.

The small parts of the install matter for decades. We install service valves on every unit so descaling is quick, route the relief valve discharge properly, build the gas connection with a sediment trap, and run venting exactly to the manufacturer's specifications. Hard water gets addressed up front with a softener or scale filter discussion, because scale is what clogs heat exchangers. We also offer recirculation if a far bathroom takes too long to get hot. Warning Signs to Watch For

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you money and keep a slow drip from turning into a flooded utility room. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Showers turning lukewarm before everyone gets a turn
  • An addition or guest suite that is a long run from the main heater
  • A water heater near the end of its service life, making this a natural time to switch
  • Preferring a heater that can be repaired and maintained instead of replaced every decade
  • Energy bills that reflect a tank heating water around the clock
  • More people or more fixtures than your current heater was sized for
  • A utility area where a bulky tank takes up room you would rather use
  • Renovation work that makes gas line and vent changes easier to fit in

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Installation?

Why not install a tankless unit yourself? Because the risks and the details stack up quickly. A burner that can draw nearly 199,000 BTU needs properly sized gas piping, and leaks or undersized lines are both dangerous and costly. Venting has to match the unit exactly, with the right material, slope, and termination clearances, or combustion gases can end up where they do not belong. Electric models may need a panel upgrade, and warranty terms often depend on a qualified installation. Permits and inspections are common requirements as well.

We take the guesswork out of going tankless. That starts with sizing for your busiest hour and coldest water, then making sure the home's gas, vent, and power can support it. We install with service valves for descaling, proper relief valve discharge, and venting built to the manual, then run the unit at full flow before we leave. If a tankless unit is not the best choice for your home, because of cost, supply limits, or how you use hot water, we say so and help you compare other options honestly.

Water Heater Care Tips From Our Technicians

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Check the vent cap outside for nests, debris, or buildup
  • Pay attention to error codes; they point to issues that are cheaper to fix early
  • Flush the unit with a descaling solution annually to keep scale off the heat exchanger
  • Keep the inlet screen clear so flow stays strong and the unit fires reliably
  • Set the temperature around 120F for safety and efficiency
  • Hard water shortens tankless life, so ask about scale protection
  • Protect outdoor or garage units from freezing, and keep power on during cold snaps so freeze protection works

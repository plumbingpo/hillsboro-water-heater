Tankless units are sophisticated machines, and that is both their strength and their weak spot. Instead of keeping forty or fifty gallons hot around the clock, a tankless heater comes to life only when water starts moving: a flow sensor detects demand, the control board fires the burner or elements, and water is heated on its way through the heat exchanger. Every step is watched by a sensor, and if any reading drifts outside its expected range, the board shuts the heater off and displays a code. For Hillsboro, OR homeowners, that usually traces back to a short list of causes: scale building up inside the heat exchanger, a sticking or dirty flow sensor, an ignition problem at the igniter or flame rod, or a venting or gas supply issue the unit reads as unsafe.

Our approach to tankless repair is simple: measure first, replace second. When we arrive at your Hillsboro home, we record the active code and the unit's fault history, then work through the systems behind it. Flow sensors are checked with water running to confirm they report demand correctly. Flame rods and igniters are tested electrically. Gas pressure is read at the inlet with the burner at maximum, because a line that holds steady at idle can sag badly under full load. The vent and air intake are inspected end to end, and the heat exchanger is judged by the temperature rise it achieves at a known flow. By the time we recommend a repair, we know it is the right one.

We work on tankless heaters from Rinnai, Navien, Noritz, Rheem, Takagi, and the other major brands, gas-fired and electric alike. The fix depends on the diagnosis: a full descaling flush through the isolation valves when scale is choking the heat exchanger, a new flow sensor when the unit no longer notices a faucet opening, a fresh igniter or cleaned flame rod when ignition keeps failing, or a vent correction when the unit trips on combustion air. Sometimes the answer is bigger. A gas line undersized for the unit's full firing rate has to be upsized, and a heat exchanger cracked by years of scale may make replacement the smarter call on an older unit. When to Call for Tankless Water Heater Repair

A heater that is starting to struggle rarely hides it for long, and the clues are easy to spot once you know them. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Moisture, rust, or drips appearing below the heater
  • No hot water at a low-flow faucet even though the shower works fine
  • The display showing an error code instead of a temperature
  • Lukewarm water whenever two fixtures run at the same time
  • Cold slugs in the middle of hot water that last longer than before
  • A clicking igniter with no flame, or a flame that lights and quickly drops out
  • A combustion fan that sounds louder or rougher than normal
  • Hot water that fluctuates even though nobody touched the faucet

Small Symptoms, Big Consequences

Tanks rarely fail all at once. They corrode from the inside for months, and the warning signs show up long before the floor gets wet. At the first hint of a problem, reach out to Hillsboro Water Heater at (503) 210-1622 and we'll take a look.

Why Hire a Professional for Tankless Water Heater Repair?

Consider the risks first. A gas-fired tankless heater can burn nearly 200,000 BTU per hour at full fire, and its vent is the only thing carrying carbon monoxide out of the house; a joint that pulls loose or an intake that gets blocked changes that in a hurry. On the electric side, the loads are large, frequently more than 100 amps spread over several 240-volt circuits. Then there is the diagnostic puzzle. Error codes point to a system, not a part, so a flow sensor fault, a scaled heat exchanger, and a bad thermistor can look nearly identical on the display, and guessing gets expensive.

What you get from a professional is certainty. Instead of swapping parts, our technicians confirm the fault with a meter, a manometer, and temperature readings, so the fix is aimed at the actual cause. Descaling is done with a pump and service valves, not by pouring vinegar into an inlet and hoping. Gas pressure is checked with the burner at full output, vent runs are inspected against the manufacturer's length and clearance limits, and the unit is tested at low and high flow before we leave. Proper service also keeps you in line with the manufacturer's warranty terms, many of which expect qualified service and regular descaling.

Simple Ways to Extend Your Water Heater's Life

A few simple habits, like an annual flush and a quick look at the relief valve, can add years to a tank. Follow these tips from our technicians:

  • Schedule a descaling flush every year; hard water may call for one every six months
  • Protect the heat exchanger with a softener or scale inhibitor when your water is hard
  • Keep the unit powered during freezing weather so its freeze protection can work
  • Tell us before adding a gas appliance, so the gas line can be checked for the extra load
  • Do not keep resetting a recurring code; note it and have it checked
  • Keep the vent termination and air intake clear of leaves, nests, snow, and lint
  • Rinse the small filter screen on the cold inlet every few months
